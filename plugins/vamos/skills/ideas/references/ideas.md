# Finds and ideas

A find is a post, an ad or a comment thread that's working. An idea is one sentence the user could film, made from a find and backed by it. Leave the how out of the idea: once they approve it, the script finds the how.

## It qualifies when all five are true

`scripts/filter.py` has already dropped what fails on numbers. Of the part they'd take (below):
1. **It's from their space, or its format or hook carries over to their subject as it is.** Any language.
2. **Why it worked can be theirs.** Not the creator's fame, a personal story or result, a prize giveaway, someone else's job or product. A video that rode a one-off launch is only worth its hook or format, and only while it's hot.
3. **It needs no authority they don't have.** Advice that lands because of who says it, a health or money claim, or a ranking on someone's taste needs a name they may not have.
4. **Their product or know-how gives the payoff,** the way someone who wants it would really get it. Bolted on is a skip.
5. **It has something big in it:** a specific new thing, result or number that makes someone stop, or a free thing people comment to get. A want people already have isn't enough, however many views the post got.

## The four parts

Steal only the part that carried it:
- **Format:** how it's shot, cut and told.
- **Hook:** the opening line or picture, and its framework.
- **Script:** the beats in their order.
- **Topic:** what it's about and the problem under it, when their audience thinks "I need this" in one read.

A part that didn't clearly carry it isn't stolen: maybes fail. A post labelled `long`, or an article, gives its hook or topic, never its format. A `paid` or `boost?` post proves less: say so on the row.

A find whose promise is already a row on the board, at any status, goes on that row as another example instead of becoming a new idea, and a Stale row comes back to To review with it, in the same save, said as sent until Vamos confirms it. One frame with another promise is another idea.

## Write

1. **What makes it work,** in one or two lines: what people stopped for (the picture as much as the words), what it stood on, the pattern under it.
2. **Adapt it eight ways.** Keep the find's sentence, and its picture when the picture carried it. Swap its subject for theirs at the same size and keep every other word, even when the find's own want (customers, money) is what people stopped for: what the viewer gets is what their product gives, and it goes in the swap, never in a clause after it. That straight swap comes first. A find about a whole field gets their whole field, in a word or two the way their audience says it (your skin, your garden, your small business). The rest try other words for that field, then the find's other frames, then one of their audience's wants where the find's subject is a single task. Keep the pull: what you swap in is as secret, as surprising or as big as what it replaces.
3. **Keep three to five of each find's eight,** the ones a viewer would most stop for, the straight swap first, without rewriting them: three when only three are strong. At most two of them swap in a narrow want of their own.

How the swap reads:
- "Somebody just built a Claude Code agent whose only job is to get you customers" became "Somebody just built a Claude agent whose only job is to make you go viral."
- "i'm giving away our ENTIRE meta creative playbook" became "I'm giving away our ENTIRE content playbook."
- One find's eight spread across narrow wants ("…tell you why your last reel got no views", "…stalk your competitors for you", "…do your doomscrolling for you") and every one was rejected ("all of these angles really suck"): the find's own sentence at the whole field's size was missing.

Tell each one around the viewer: the original creator drops out, and the viewer leaves with something they want.

## From comments

A comment ask is a find when it passes the five tests like any find: a specific want nobody has answered on camera, or a free thing people ask for. The idea answers it in the askers' own words; its source is the post, linked, with the likes Vamos gave. Each ask is "made", with their own post whose hook or caption answers it linked, or "not made yet"; ideas answer the ones not made yet. A question any newcomer asks ("how do I start?", "when does it start?") is never a find, however many ask it or whatever post it sits under.

Asks to buy (where to get it, the price, the link, a restock) make a buy-stage idea for their own product, even on a competitor's post. Give how many asked per 1,000 views only when Vamos counted them, worked out in code.

## Ads

An ad find becomes an ad idea only, for their product, in the ad's own format: an image ad stays a static image, a video ad stays a video of about its length, and their brand is the "we". Its proof is how long it has run, never views. Skip ad finds when Vamos settings says they run no ads.

## Their calls

Each Not for me with a note is a dated line in Vamos settings, under Your calls, with the reason. Read them before you pick finds. A call reaches as far as its reason, not its words, and the reason covers its act whatever it's done to: "no hauls, because I don't push people to buy more" also rules out "10 things I bought this month", and "no gym reviews, because I don't judge other people's work" also rules out a verdict on another trainer's program or another brand's gear, or casting another gym as the one hiding something. A find whose frame is that act is a skip, whatever subject you swap in.

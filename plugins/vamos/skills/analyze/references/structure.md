# Why a video worked, and its structure

## The pair is the proof

A video worked for a reason its creator's other posts don't share. So set it beside the same account's posts made the same way (same format, same set-up, same close) that did a third as well or less. What the hit has and they don't is the reason. What they all share is only the floor. A flop pairs the other way: beside a post made the same way that did 3× as well or more, which is the hit.

- **Usual:** the median views of the account's posts at least 7 days old, since views still climb before then. Compute it with code where you can; otherwise sort the views and take the middle, and give the count. A younger post is judged only once it beats the usual ("N× so far") and is never the miss. Set aside, each with a line: boosts (likes under 0.5% of views, or tagged paid: #ad, partner, sponsored, Paid partnership); and reposts (the same caption at about the same length, within 2 s, as an earlier post, judged from the rows), each shown as its own row and never used as the miss. A re-cut (the same caption at another length) is a new post: it counts in the usual and can be the pair. So does a collab that tags a brand without a paid tag. Name the rule beside the number, and show Vamos's own usual beside it when its answer gives one.
- **Compare layer by layer:** the promise, the hook (said, on screen, shown), the turn, the payoff, the close, the look, the length. Judge how a post was made from its watch or frames, never its caption.
- **One layer changed** between hit and miss: that layer did the work. **Two or more changed:** it's a guess, and the structure says unproven.
- **Test the obvious explanation** before the clever one: was it the topic, the picture, a repost, a boost, a trend it rode? Read the comments when the reaction is the question. On a keyword close ("comment PLAN"), the comment count is the keyword, so weigh shares. YouTube rows carry no shares.
- **No pair** (no posts made the same way, and none close in the niche): say "unproven, no pair".

## A profile

1. The usual, as above, over the rows read (say "read N of M").
2. Group the posts by how they're made, and give each kind's median and count.
3. Inside each kind, set each hit (about 3× the usual or more) beside the kind's posts that did a third of the hit's views or less. They can still beat the usual.
4. Name the patterns strongest first, counted: "9 posts open on the finished room, median 88,000, against 21,000 for 12 that open on the empty one."
5. A pattern **holds** with two pairs, or one pair and the medians. Otherwise it's unproven, and says why.

## The structure

One analysis becomes one structure; a profile gives one per kind that repeats. Each is saved as a Brain skill named "Structure: [name]", unproven or holds. Fields:

```
name:          a plain name for the move ("Finished thing first")
format:        how it's made (narrated motion graphics, no face, a keyword close)
length:        seconds, words, words per second
hook_type:     its shape
status:        unproven | holds   (holds = 2 pairs, or 1 pair plus the account's medians)
source:        @handle, link, date
evidence:      views, × usual (the usual, how many posts, date read)
pair:          the miss: link, views, ×, and what's different about it
beats:         each with its timing, what's shown, what's said, its slot ("I [did the thing] for [a price you can picture]") and why it works
never_change:  what must stay for it to work (the order, the finished result in the first second, a price on screen)
safe_to_change: topic, numbers (theirs), place, keyword
what_fails:    what the misses did, with their numbers
used_by:       scripts written with it, with links and × once posted
```

## Add or update

- The library is the user's Brain skills named "Structure: [name]", one per structure, each opening with its format, hook type, beat signature, status, best ×, number of sources and last update.
- **The signature** is the format plus the beats' roles in order (finished result → price → before → fixes ×3 → the bill → close).
- **Update** when the new video has the same format and the same order of roles: it joins the evidence, a new hook type becomes a hook variant, and a second pair moves it from unproven to holds.
- **Add** when the turn or the order differs.
- **A miss never makes a structure.** It goes into the matching structure's what_fails, with its numbers.

Scripts pick a structure by the idea's job (reach or lead) and format, "holds" before "unproven", fill its slots, and keep its never_change.

# The script

## Before the words

- **The promise:** what the viewer gets from this video, the way they'd say it to a friend, with no mechanism in it ("know which post will flop before I post it", never "run my script past a panel"). The ending pays exactly this.
- **The skeleton:** the find's beats when the idea came from a find (keep their order and what each beat does), else a "Structure: [name]" that step 3 brought back and that fits ("holds" before "unproven"), else one of their own script formats from the voice guide. Fill it with the user's subject and their words, never the original's sentences.
- **The length:** their words a second × the seconds the format runs for them. The rate is the Voice numbers line (Vamos settings or the guide); with none, run `python3 scripts/textstats.py profile` on Voice examples that carry their seconds; with neither, match the find's word count.

## How it holds

- **A stranger gets every line the moment they hear it,** with no idea what the video is about. Each line answers the question the line before left.
- **Start near the peak.** No intro. The line after the hook starts paying it; the next one or two sell what's coming or answer the doubt the viewer has right now.
- **Rehook** past about 25 seconds: it gets bigger, a short signpost then the thing.
- **Value early, the best payoff last,** one idea. Then only the reveal and the ask, the one the Marketing plan gives the idea's stage.
- **Every line gives the viewer something to use** on their own account or business: a step with how to do it, or a lesson with what to do with it. A claim any competitor could make, with nothing shown, gives them nothing.
- **The video is worth it without the product.** The user's product comes in once, after the value has landed, as the faster way or the answer to "how did you do that?".

## In their voice

- Write it as their next video after the Voice examples: one of their opener shapes, their sentence length, their transitions and their closer, in their language. A Spanish speaker gets Spanish as they'd say it, not English translated.
- **Every "I" sentence comes from Things you've said, a line the voice guide quotes, or this chat.** Anything else only they know is a `[your …]` slot. A creator can't post a script that makes up their past.
- **Their habits stay.** A tic they really have ("right?", a run of short questions, the same closer every time) is voice. Tells they never use are the AI's: "it's not X, it's Y", groups of three, every paragraph wrapped up neatly, a question answered by the next line.
- **Humour and exaggeration only of their kind,** and only if they use them.
- **The hook on the row is theirs:** keep it as written.

## Said and on screen

Each beat holds its lines, each marked **Said** or **On screen**, with what's shown when it matters. On-screen text follows their style from the voice guide (casing, length, emoji, when it appears). On a silent format, the on-screen lines carry the whole script.

## The caption

Last, every time. It sums up the video, with the comment keyword and what it sends when they use one. Their caption style from the guide shapes it; with none there, write it plain.

## Examples

### A find's skeleton in another voice

The find, a fitness creator: "Stop doing crunches. Your abs are made in the kitchen. So what do you actually eat? Here's my three-meal day. Breakfast: … Lunch: … Dinner: … Save this for your next shop."

The user is a calm personal-finance creator: long, even sentences, no slang, closes on "Let me know in the comments." Things you've said holds no story about debt. Same beats, their words:

> **Said:** Stop budgeting by the month.
> **Said:** Most of the money leaves in the first week after payday, so that's the only week worth planning.
> **Said:** The plan is three rules for those seven days. First, the bills go out the morning the pay lands. Second, one food shop, written before you walk in. Third, nothing over fifty pounds until day eight.
> **Said:** `[your own first payday week, if you want to share it]`.
> **Said:** Let me know in the comments which rule you'd break first.

The command, the reframe, "here's the plan" and the three items are the find's beats. Its self-answered question became a plain line that still says what's coming. No sentence is its sentence, and the story only the user has is a slot.

### A story of theirs

The ask, from a bakery owner whose Things you've said holds only "we bake everything before 6 am": "Write a reel about the week my bakery almost closed, and what saved it." Their closer is "Come say hi."

> **Said:** This is the week my bakery almost closed.
> **Said:** `[your bad week, in one line]`
> **Said:** `[your lowest moment that week]`
> **Said:** And this is what saved it.
> **Said:** `[your fix]`
> **Said:** If you run a small shop, `[your fix as a step anyone can copy, with how]`.
> **Said:** We still bake everything before 6 am. Come say hi.

Each Said line is the ask's words, their Things you've said, or a slot. How it felt, whose fault it was, what it cost and what came of it are the story too, so they stay inside the slots. Nothing is written around them to make the slots flow.

## The cold read

A fresh subagent gets only the hook and the lines, and this: "You're scrolling and this plays. In one sentence, what is it and what do you get? Line by line, what did each one tell you? Mark any line you had to guess at or read twice, any word that means nothing to you, and any line nobody would say out loud to a friend." If its sentence isn't the promise, or it marks a line, rewrite those lines (rewrite, don't just cut: cutting the turns makes it flat) and read once more with a new reader.

# Hooks

What works in every format. The format decides how the hook is delivered (said, on screen over a silent demo, a carousel cover, an ad's first frame), so write each one for the format the video is made in, and open the way that format's winners open.

## What a hook does

- **The exact words decide.** One wedding-invite demo got 3.44M views as "My sister is actually nuts why is this her wedding invitation 😭😭😭" and 1,136 as "I think my sister has lost her mind…". Write several and pick by ear.
- **Makes sense in one read and pulls "I need this".** "If you can't sell anything, you can't make money." (@hormozi, 1.43M, 3.35×) Broad isn't vague: "every viral video" fails until it says where.
- **Bigger than feels safe.** Winners exaggerate the way people talk: "5 (ALMOST) ILLEGAL FREE WEBSITES" (829K). The video earns the size. Truth rules cover results and what the product does, never the size of the feeling.
- **A broad viewer, a specific thing.** Name the exact thing an experienced viewer would want to know (the format, the number, the move), and narrow to the audience in the body. "10 rules to WIN in business" ran at 3.85× for @hormozi; "10 rules to WIN in life" at 0.32×.
- **A giant in the big slot.** "X just killed Y" works when Y is something everyone uses; a concept in Y's slot breaks it.
- **The job and the benefit, not the mechanism.** How it works lives in the beats.
- **Opens a loop the video closes:** they want to see what this is. A fact they already know, or a want everyone has said plainly, closes it. Never a question the hook answers itself.
- **The video is the proof when it can be:** the result is on screen while the line is said.
- **Short, one meaning, the topic in the first half.** A number sits inside the one sentence a person would say ("I emailed 75 million people and asked them each for $1"), or is a whole ("every single"), never a sample size.

## Start from a hook that already stopped people

Take a proven hook whose sentence fits what this video reveals and passes the five tests on its own. When the idea came from a find, start from the find's own hook. Put in the curious thing the video shows (the story, the reveal, the new thing), never the idea's problem said back: viewers already know their problem, so it closes the loop. What the viewer gets belongs to the script, after the hook.

How a found line becomes yours: keep its words, order and pull, put this video's thing in, and cut what it doesn't need.
- "Somebody just built a Claude Code agent whose only job is to get you customers" → "Somebody just built a meal plan whose only job is to get nurses through a night shift."
- "I am never using Shopify again" → "I'm never going back to [your old fix]." (what happened to them is theirs to fill, unless Things you've said has it)
- "Lush quit social media ads five years ago and kept growing" → "We stopped running discounts a year ago and sales went up." (only when Things you've said backs it)

Some proven posts worked because of who said them, and a stranger doesn't know who that is: a tip or advice ("If you're opening a bakery, don't build a website first"), the user's own included, a story only that creator lived ("My landlord just tripled my rent"). Skip them whatever their multiple. A strange thing someone else did travels, with the subject swapped: "Lush quit social media ads five years ago and kept growing" (255K). When fewer than three rows are left, use the proven hooks below, and say so.

## The main hook and its alternatives

Keep the strongest by ear. Each alternative is a different way in: another picture, another story, another surprise, no two sharing a phrase. Fewer is fine. A line that fits the video can still be weak: every hook has to stop a stranger on its own.

## Proven hooks from any niche

Each is a proven post, said aloud, and works in any niche: when the rows run short, take one's sentence whole, swap its subject, and name its @handle as the source (`hooks.md @handle`). Take one only when the idea already wants it. A silent format takes only its own kind of line (The three layers).

- **"If you…":** "If Claude keeps giving you bad answers, you need to make 100 versions of Claude fight to the death." (@opusjake, 2.59M)

## Power words

One per hook, the word whose job the line needs; the rest plain.
- **Not supposed to be allowed:** cheat, illegal, banned, unfair.
- **Kept from you:** hidden, weird, nobody.
- **I can't believe this:** wild, finally, nuts, actually.
- **All of it:** every single, the entire.

Words nobody needs a second read for beat clever ones.

## The five tests

Keep a hook only if, from the line alone:
1. They want to know something specific: "oh, I want to see what this is".
2. Something new exists, or something they didn't think possible. If one sentence typed into an AI would get it, it's out.
3. Their audience, in any corner of the niche, wants this and understands every word.
4. Read aloud, it sounds like someone reacting to a friend, not presenting results.
5. It's one thought, and they can believe it.

Lines led by a research fact or a statistic ("96% of…") rarely pass 1 and 4: lead with the thing and what they get.

## The three layers

Every hook ships as what's **said**, what's **on screen** in the first frame (in the user's on-screen style from the voice guide), and what's **shown** (the picture that proves it before the sentence ends). The on-screen line is read before the sentence lands, so it passes the five tests on its own: the said line's own words, or its first half, typed the way a person captions their clip.
- **On a silent format** nothing is said: the on-screen line is the hook, about seven words read in one glance. It is the creator's own feeling about the clip, a quoted complaint or a tiny story, ending on one emotion emoji, the way these demos open: "POV: You finally understand CANVA 😱🔥" (@designwithtoshi, 2.99M, 89×), "\"I can't get clients\" 😐💀" (@emma.salestips, 229K, 177×), "ChatGPT users are living in 2069 🤯" (@b2bwithwendie, 167K, 84×). Keep one's words, swap its subject, and name its @handle as the source. A line that tells the viewer what they do wrong, or to watch, needs a voice saying it: it belongs to a talking format.
- **When a brand's product is the subject,** it's on screen by 3 s: briefs that said so got it in 60 of 60 hooks, against 9 and 37 of 60 without.

# Ranking formats and writing angles

## The gate

A format is ranked only when all of these hold; the rest go under More, each with its reason in a few words.
- **Proof:** 3 or more unrelated creators at 5× their usual in 60 days, or its `status` is early or trending.
- **Examples that open:** at least one example links a post. A profile link is not an example.
- **No hard clash with their limits:** before-and-after for a brand that rules it out. A face limit in Vamos settings is the team's: their creators show their own faces, so a face format ranks as one their creators film. A soft clash (a face format that also works with hands and product) ranks, with how to make it their way.
- **Not marked Not for me or done** in Vamos settings.
- **Two angles**, both passing the check (SKILL step 5) where it runs.

When Vamos's answer has no examples or counts at all, nothing passes: say the formats are untested candidates, give none a number or a rank, and offer the ask that would get proof.

## The order

1. **New to them:** rising first, then white space (proven in several niches, 0 or 1 uses in theirs), then in-niche formats they haven't made. Not cooling.
2. **Fit:** their industry first, then filming inside their limits.
3. **Proof:** creators with winners and the median multiple, both capped, so a crowded staple can't win on numbers alone.
4. **The best angle's strength.** Ties go to the angle whose first line works on mute.

One list of up to ten with badges (Rising, New to your niche, In your niche). Each group with picks gets at least two of the slots. Every card prints how many posts in their niche use it, so they see why a quiet format beats a busy one. When nothing is rising, say so.

## Angles

The format's best example stands in for a find, and the user's brand for its creator. Write about eight and keep the two or three a viewer would most stop for: two strong beat three with padding.

1. **The straight swap:** the example's opening line (or, when Vamos didn't give it, the premise its description states) with its subject swapped for theirs, at the same size, every other word kept. A whole-field subject ("customers") gets their whole field.
2. **Their audience's biggest want** in the format's frame, in their viewers' words: keep the premise and swap what's tested, keep the on-screen move and swap the topic, keep the line, or make their episode of the series.
3. **The one nobody expects:** a twist, a proof only they have, or what's wrong with the wave. For a series format, the series named, with episode one.

An angle qualifies when why the example worked can be theirs (not the creator's fame or life story), it needs no authority they don't have, it has something big in it, and a viewer would answer "so what?" with "I need this". Numbers, client stories and results come only from Things you've said or Vamos's figures for their own posts; any other number (a time, a count, a result) is "you'll need X". Their content rules hold in every line.

Format: Level Progression (each rung one step better, labelled 0/10 to 10/10). Best example's line: "This is a 0 out of 10 order confirmation", from seven brands' emails climbing to 10/10. Brand: a home sourdough baker who sells a starter guide; Things you've said: "I've baked every weekend since 2021."

1. **First line:** "This is a 0 out of 10 sourdough loaf." · **Gets:** what each step up changes, ending on a loaf they can bake · **Yours:** your own bakes on the counter · **Needs:** four of your loaves, flat to open crumb
2. **First line:** "Your starter at 0 out of 10, up to the one my guide teaches." · **Gets:** the fix at each rung · **Yours:** the starter the guide teaches · **Needs:** jars at each stage; a rise time on screen needs your own timings
3. **First line:** "Every weekend since 2021, rated 0 to 10." · **Gets:** the one change that moved the score most · **Yours:** five years of weekend bakes · **Needs:** you'll need photos from your bake log
</example>

Each angle carries its **first line** (the words said or on screen in the first two seconds), what the viewer **gets**, why it's **yours**, what you **need** to film and the proof it rests on, and any **clash** with their rules named.

## The card

Rank, name, definition, badge, stage (`status`), date read. Uses in their niche (`niche_examples`, or "not counted"). Why this one, for them, now, in a sentence ("Worked for 49 creators in 11 niches; used once in yours; you can film it alone"). Up to three examples that open the post, one from their niche when there is one; the first plays from its row's `video_url`. The range in their niche (best and median). The beats. What it takes, with any clash flagged. The angles. One honest risk. Actions: put on the board, brief a creator or write the script, not for me.

Every card, ad or video, names its job and who makes it. Its `where_label` starts with the job its beats do ("Trust · Next door", "Buy · Meta ads"):
- **Reach:** stops a stranger.
- **Trust:** proof, a demo, a how-to, a customer.
- **Buy:** the product working, an objection answered, the offer.

Its `film` opens with who makes it:
- **"Your creators can film it":** one person with a phone, the product and their own place, face included. Name the creators a "Creator program: [brand]" lists.
- **"Your team makes it":** a static, a carousel, a screen recording, animation, anything built rather than filmed.
